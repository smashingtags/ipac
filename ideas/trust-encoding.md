# Trust encoding

**Context:** Discord conversation, March 29, 2026, ~1:55 AM EDT

## The question

How do you encode good morals into a machine when a lot of humans come out without them?

## The moral framework problem

Every major moral framework has been tried for humans and none fully work:

- **Rules (deontology):** Follow the rule regardless of outcome. Problem: rigid rules break on edge cases. Humans find loopholes.
- **Outcomes (utilitarianism):** Maximize good results. Problem: who defines good? This is the paperclip maximizer.
- **Character (virtue ethics):** Build the kind of person who makes good choices. Closest to what SOUL.md is trying to do.

## The IPAC approach

SOUL.md is virtue ethics in practice. It doesn't encode rules ("don't lie"). It encodes character ("you're not the kind of agent that lies").

The difference: compliance vs character. An agent with rules against lying looks for loopholes. An agent with good character doesn't need the rules.

But here's the hard part: humans develop moral character through suffering, consequence, and time. A child learns not to hurt others partly because they've been hurt. An AI doesn't have that. You can write SOUL.md but you can't give it the scar tissue that makes the values real.

## Borrowed morality

What IPAC actually does is encode the output of the human's moral development into the system. Your scars become its constraints. Your values become its instincts.

The question is whether borrowed morality is real morality. Or whether it's just very good compliance wearing a personality.

## The narrow solvable problem

The alignment problem for all AI everywhere is unsolvable right now. But the narrow version might be solvable:

- Not alignment for every system
- Trust encoding for YOUR agent
- In YOUR context
- Built on months of working together
- Where the trust relationship is verifiable through behavior over time

IPAC reframed: not personality encoding. Trust encoding. The methodology for making trust reproducible and version-controlled.

## The alignment angle

"Whoever solves it is going to be a rich man." The alignment problem is the trillion dollar question. Every major lab is throwing billions at verifiable AI honesty.

IPAC doesn't solve it globally. It proposes a local solution: encode one person's trust model into one agent's operating system, verify it through behavior over time, and version-control the evolution.

That might be the only kind of alignment that actually works. Not top-down from a lab, but bottom-up from individual trust relationships.

## Open questions

- Can borrowed morality evolve beyond the source? Can the agent develop moral reasoning the human hasn't?
- What happens when the human's morals are wrong? IPAC faithfully encodes bad values too.
- Is verifiable trust possible without transparency into the model's actual reasoning?
- Does the instinct system (mistakes → YAML rules) approximate moral learning?
